Statement of Purpose
I am Suha Ferdous, a university lecturer of English at IUBAT, Bangladesh. I have three years of experience teaching diverse groups of students. My academic background includes a B.A. and an M.A. in English from the University of Dhaka, which has shaped my passion for teaching and improving English language education in resource-constrained environments. I am now eager to expand my expertise in my field. The Master of Education in Teaching English to Speakers of Other Languages (TESOL) at the University of Exeter is a program that aligns seamlessly with my aspirations to become a transformative educator and researcher in this field.
To me, the University of Exeter stands out for its research-led teaching, global reputation in education studies, and focus on equipping educators to navigate complex teaching contexts. The MEd TESOL program’s blend of theoretical exploration and practical application particularly resonates with my desire to bridge gaps in English language teaching methodologies in Bangladesh. The program’s core modules, such as “Language Awareness for TESOL” and “Developing Practical Knowledge for TESOL Teaching,” promise to strengthen my foundational understanding of linguistics and its application in classroom practice. Moreover, modules like “Educational Research in TESOL” and “Foreign Language Testing and Assessment” will allow me to hone my research skills and design effective assessment strategies tailored to my students’ needs.
The program’s commitment to fostering intercultural understanding through its diverse student body from countries such as China, South Korea, and Mexico is a particularly compelling feature. Having worked with students from various socio-economic backgrounds, I understand how cultural sensitivity and awareness can enrich teaching practices. This multicultural environment at Exeter will provide me with invaluable insights into global TESOL challenges and solutions, further refining my approach to teaching in Bangladesh’s multilingual and multicultural context.
At IUBAT, I have witnessed firsthand the transformative power of language education in empowering students from underprivileged communities. These experiences have underscored the need for evidence-based, context-sensitive teaching practices that address the unique challenges faced by learners in developing countries. My long-term goal is to design training programs for EFL teachers in Bangladesh, equipping them with innovative strategies that leverage technology, promote learner autonomy, and foster inclusivity. Exeter’s focus on practical teaching and research will give me the tools to achieve this vision.
Furthermore, the MEd TESOL program’s emphasis on the intersection of research and practice is particularly relevant to my interest in exploring the role of digital tools in enhancing learner engagement. With the global shift toward technology-driven education, especially post-pandemic, there is an urgent need to understand how digital innovations can be adapted for diverse classroom settings. I plan to focus my dissertation on investigating the effectiveness of such tools in Bangladesh, a country with growing internet penetration but limited access to advanced educational technologies.
Exeter’s supportive learning environment, with access to the university’s well-equipped research facilities and expert faculty, will provide me with the mentorship and resources needed to refine my academic and professional skills. I am also drawn to the program’s focus on developing reflective practitioners who can adapt to evolving educational landscapes—a quality that I strive to embody in my teaching career.
After completing this program, I aim to spearhead initiatives that combine research, policy-making, and teacher training in Bangladesh. My goal is to collaborate with academic institutions and governmental bodies to design curricula that incorporate global best practices while addressing local challenges. In the long run, I hope to contribute to academic scholarship in TESOL by publishing research on effective strategies for English language teaching in developing nations.
In conclusion, the University of Exeter’s MEd TESOL program is the perfect platform for me to deepen my expertise, broaden my global perspective, and achieve my aspirations. I am confident that the program’s rigorous curriculum, diverse academic community, and emphasis on research-driven practice will enable me to make a lasting impact on English language education in my country and beyond.
Thank you for considering my application.
Sincerely,
Suha Ferdous
